Screening for Depression in Palliative Cancer Patients Attending a Pain and Symptom Control Clinic
Rami A. Sela
Department of Psychosocial and Spiritual Resources, Cross Cancer Institute, Department of Oncology, Faculty of Medicine and Dentistry, University of Alberta, Edmonton, Alberta, Canada. Published 2007 — Palliative & Supportive Care, Vol. 5, No. 3, pp. 207-217, doi: 10.1017/S1478951507000375. Published online 24 Sep 2007.
132 oncology outpatients attending a multidisciplinary pain and symptom control clinic at the Cross Cancer Institute, Edmonton, Alberta, Canada; cross-sectional design (single-visit screening survey); mixed cancer types (distribution not specified in available sources); 52.3% female (per source review table).

Instruments:
Brief Zung Self-Rating Depression Scale (BZSDS; Dugan et al., 1998) — abbreviated version of the Zung Self-Rating Depression Scale (SDS), used as the primary depression measure; scored into ranges of "mild," "moderate," and "severe" depressive symptoms
Depression Visual Analogue Scale (DVAS) — single-item visual analogue scale, evaluated in this study as a brief alternative screening tool; correlated with the BZSDS at r = 0.82

variables:
sociodemographic ->
sex (52.3% female per source review table; other sociodemographic variables not specified in available sources)

clinical/medical status ->
cancer diagnosis (mixed cancer types, distribution not specified in available sources); attendance at a pain and symptom control clinic (i.e., sample drawn from a symptomatic/advanced outpatient population, not necessarily a formal palliative care unit)

outcome variables ->
Depression (BZSDS; categorized as clinically significant vs. not, and further into mild/moderate/severe)
Depression (DVAS) — evaluated for concurrent validity against the BZSDS, not as an independent outcome

{
Results (from published abstract):
The majority of participants (72%) showed clinically significant depressive symptoms on the BZSDS, split as 21% "mild," 32% "moderate," and 19% "severe."
Participants showed low endorsement of items reflecting overt manifestations of depression (e.g., sadness, tearfulness, irritability, suicidal ideation) — suggesting depression in this population may present atypically or be underrecognized through observable affect alone, consistent with the article's stated concern about underrecognition of depression in palliative care.
The DVAS correlated strongly with the BZSDS (r = 0.82), supporting its use as a simple, quick screening tool in this population.

Key findings for model training:
- One of the earliest studies in the corpus (2007) and one of the only ones to explicitly compare two screening instruments (a multi-item scale, BZSDS, vs. a single-item visual analogue scale, DVAS) for detecting depression — directly relevant if ACOMPANAR is considering brief/single-item emotional-state screening as an alternative to longer questionnaires.
- The finding that patients showed LOW endorsement of overt/observable depressive manifestations (sadness, tearfulness, irritability, suicidal ideation) despite HIGH overall depressive symptom burden is an important and somewhat counterintuitive signal: it suggests depression in advanced cancer patients may manifest more through somatic/less-visible symptoms than through outwardly recognizable affect — a caution against relying on observable emotional expression alone as a proxy for depression risk.
- Strong correlation (r = 0.82) between a single-item visual analogue scale and a full multi-item depression scale suggests that brief, low-burden screening tools can capture much of the same signal as longer instruments in this population — potentially useful for lightweight, low-friction check-ins in a conversational/companion tool context.
- 72% prevalence is on the higher end of this corpus (range ~10-88%), consistent with a symptomatic outpatient population attending a pain and symptom control clinic (i.e., likely higher symptom burden than a general oncology outpatient sample).

Limitations (inferred / reported):
- Cross-sectional design — cannot assess how depression screening results relate to later clinical outcomes or change over time.
- Single-center sample (one clinic in Edmonton, Alberta) — limits generalizability.
- Sample specifically drawn from a pain and symptom control clinic — likely biased toward patients with higher symptom burden than a general oncology or palliative care population; prevalence figures may not generalize to less symptomatic advanced cancer populations.
- Full text not accessible for this extraction — detailed statistical tables and any adjustment for confounding variables could not be verified independently; entry is based on abstract-level data and the source review table only.
