Article #21
Magnitude of Anxiety and Depression and Associated Factors among Palliative Care Patients with Cancer at Tikur Anbessa Specialized Hospital, Ethiopia
Bantalem Tilaye Atinafu, Tefera Mulugeta Demlew, Fetene Nigussie Tarekegn
Ethiopia, published March 2022 — Ethiop J Health Sci. 32(2):331-342, doi: 10.4314/ejhs.v32i2.14
171 palliative care patients with cancer, Black Lion Specialized Hospital (Tikur Anbessa) oncology center, Addis Ababa (institution-based cross-sectional study, March 9 - April 9, 2020)

Instrument:
Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) — anxiety and depression subscales, 4-point scale (0-3) per item, total score 0-21 per subscale
Cutoff used in this study: scores 0-8 = absence of symptoms, ≥8-21 = presence of symptoms (note: cutoff described in the article as inverted relative to standard HADS convention — as written, 0 to 8 = presence, 8 to 21 = absence; text of Methods section is internally ambiguous, but Results/Discussion treat higher scores as indicating presence of anxiety/depression, consistent with standard HADS usage)
Internal consistency (Ethiopian validation): anxiety α=0.78, depression α=0.76, full scale α=0.87
Data collection: structured interview questionnaire + chart review (used due to symptom burden limiting patients' ability to independently report cancer type, treatment, diagnosis length, comorbidities)

variables:
sociodemographic ->
gender, age (<44, 44-64, >64), marital status, residence (urban/rural), monthly income (Ethiopian birr), educational status, occupational status

clinico-pathological ->
tumor type, duration of diagnosis (<12 months, ≥12 months), comorbidity (yes/no), intervention/treatment received (chemotherapy, surgery+chemo, radiation+surgery+chemo, surgery+radiation, other)

outcome variables ->
HADS anxiety (presence/absence)
HADS depression (presence/absence)

Overall prevalence:
Anxiety: 64.9% (n=111/171, 95% CI: 56.7-72.5%)
Depression: 47.4% (n=81/171, 95% CI: 40.0-54.4%)

Key findings for model training:
- Age >64 is a strong independent risk factor for anxiety (AOR 7.1) — consistent with hypothesis that older age increases disease duration, metastasis probability, and disability
- Lower education level (illiterate, secondary school) associated with LOWER anxiety risk relative to college/university-educated patients — counterintuitive; authors suggest higher education may correlate with greater awareness of disease severity/prognosis, increasing anxiety
- Breast cancer diagnosis associated with markedly LOWER depression risk vs. lung cancer (AOR 0.1) — tumor type is a meaningful differentiator
- Treatment modality matters: surgery+radiation (without chemo) associated with lower depression risk vs. chemotherapy alone (AOR 0.2) — possibly due to fewer treatment-related side effects/greater familiarity
- Occupational status significant for depression at bivariate level (p=0.015) but not retained as significant in final multivariate model
- Comorbidity, residence, marital status, monthly income, and duration of diagnosis were NOT significant factors for either anxiety or depression in this cohort
- Both anxiety (64.9%) and depression (47.4%) prevalence rates are notably higher than most international comparators cited (New York 7.6% anxiety; Michigan 32.4% depression; Malaysia 22-31.7%; Singapore 9.5-16.8%), but broadly consistent with other African studies (Rwanda 67.7% anxiety; Kenya 42.0% depression) — suggests regional/cultural and psychosocial-support-infrastructure factors are relevant covariates, not just clinical ones
- Authors attribute cross-national discrepancies partly to differing screening tools/cutoffs (HADS vs. PHQ-9 vs. BDI-II vs. DSM criteria) — a methodological consideration for combining prevalence estimates across studies in training data

Limitations noted by authors:
- Cross-sectional design — cannot establish causation
- Quantitative only — no qualitative parameters captured
- Anxiety/depression classified as binary presence/absence, which may overestimate prevalence compared to continuous severity scoring
- Single-center study (Black Lion Specialized Hospital, Addis Ababa) — may limit generalizability
- COVID-19 pandemic context noted as a potential period-specific confounder affecting psychological state of comorbid cancer patients
