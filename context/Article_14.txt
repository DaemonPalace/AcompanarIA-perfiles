Article #14
Associations of nutrition impact symptoms with depression in patients with advanced cancer
Koji Amano, Satomi Okamura, Yoshinobu Matsuda, Vickie E. Baracos, Naoharu Mori, Tomofumi Miura, Ryohei Tatara, Takaomi Kessoku, Keita Tagami, Hiroyuki Otani, Masanori Mori, Tomohiko Taniyama, Nobuhisa Nakajima, Erika Nakanishi, Jun Kako, Tatsuya Morita & Mitsunori Miyashita
Japan, published 19 June 2024 — Supportive Care in Cancer, Volume 32, article number 445
225 patients (secondary analysis of a multicenter survey; 11 hospitals, palliative care outpatient services, hospital palliative care teams, and palliative care units in Japan; validation phase, Jan–Jul 2021)

Instruments:
Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) — depression screening, cutoff ≥10 = major depression
Ingesta-Verbal/Visual Analog Scale (Ingesta-VVAS) — dietary intake, 0-10 scale, higher = better intake
19 Nutrition Impact Symptoms (NISs) — 0-10 scale each (0 none; 1-3 mild; 4-6 moderate; 7-9 severe; 10 unbearable), adapted from Edmonton Symptom Assessment System and Patient-Generated Subjective Global Assessment
Functional Assessment of Anorexia/Cachexia Therapy Anorexia Cachexia Subscale (FAACT ACS) — 12 items (5-item anorexia symptoms + 4-item anorexia concerns), higher = better QOL
ECOG Performance Status
International cachexia diagnostic criteria (6-month %WL >5%, or BMI <20 kg/m2 + 6-month %WL >2%)

variables:
sociodemographic ->
age, sex

clinical / disease ->
primary cancer site, ECOG performance status (0-1, 2, 3, 4), symptomatic fluid retention (edema, pleural effusion, ascites), cachexia/refractory cachexia status, treatment status (pre-chemotherapy, chemotherapy, never treated/previous treatment)

anthropometric ->
height, current and previous body weight, BMI, 6-month weight loss rate (%WL)

nutrition impact symptoms (19 items, 0-10 scale) ->
oral pain, pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, drowsiness, lack of appetite, early satiety, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, abnormal taste, abnormal smell, dry mouth, dental problems, difficulty swallowing, food bolus obstruction, anxiety, feeling sad

outcome variables ->
PHQ-9 score (dichotomized <10 non-depression / ≥10 depression)
number of NISs with a score ≥4 (categorized: 0, 1-3, 4-6, 7 or more)
dietary intake (Ingesta-VVAS)
FAACT ACS 12-item, 5-item anorexia symptoms, 4-item anorexia concerns scores

Overall prevalence of depression (PHQ-9 ≥10): 34.2% (n=77/225)
Non-depression group: n=148 (65.8%)

Key findings for model training:
- Depression prevalence in this palliative care population (34.2%) is notably higher than in general cancer populations (8-24% per meta-analysis of 211 studies), likely reflecting proximity to end of life and broader depression spectrum
- Having ≥4 NISs with a score ≥4 is the strongest identified risk marker for depression — dose-response relationship (4-6 NISs: adjusted OR 10.76; ≥7 NISs: adjusted OR 17.02)
- Female sex independently associated with depression in adjusted model (OR 2.34, p=0.026) despite non-significance in crude analysis
- ECOG performance status and treatment status lost significance after adjustment for NIS burden — suggesting NIS count may mediate much of their association with depression
- Symptomatic fluid retention significantly associated with depression at bivariate level (p=0.007)
- Strongest individual NIS associations with depression (p<0.001): lack of appetite, early satiety, fatigue, drowsiness, nausea, abnormal smell, dry mouth, anxiety, feeling sad
- No significant NIS associations: oral pain, diarrhea, difficulty swallowing, food bolus obstruction
- Depression correlated with significantly worse cachexia-related QOL across all FAACT ACS subscales (p<0.001)
- Proposed mechanism: shared pathogenesis between cancer cachexia and depression via CNS/HPA axis dysregulation and systemic inflammation — NISs and emotional distress mutually amplify each other

Limitations noted by authors:
- Cross-sectional design — no causality can be established, confounders may be unaccounted for
- Cachexia status excluded from regression model due to high prevalence of symptomatic fluid retention (23%) potentially impairing diagnostic criteria accuracy
- No data on prior depression diagnosis or antidepressant use
- Patients with major depressive disorder may have been excluded by study exclusion criteria (serious psychological distress)
- No data on systemic inflammation markers or specific treatment modality (cytotoxic chemo, targeted therapy, immunotherapy)
- Single-country (Japan) setting may limit generalizability
