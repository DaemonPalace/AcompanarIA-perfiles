Article #25
Assessing anxiety and depression with respect to the quality of life in cancer inpatients receiving palliative care
Radka Bužgová, Darja Jarošová, Erika Hajnová
Czech Republic (Ostrava), published online 2015 — European Journal of Oncology Nursing, doi: 10.1016/j.ejon.2015.04.006
225 advanced cancer inpatients, University Hospital Ostrava (cross-sectional observational study with a 21-day repeated measurement in a subgroup of 140 patients; data collected January-December 2013)

Instruments:
Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) — Czech version, validated in this study (Cronbach's alpha: HADS-a 0.80, HADS-d 0.79; test-retest r: HADS-a 0.89, HADS-d 0.75); 14 items (7 anxiety/HADS-a, 7 depression/HADS-d), each 0-3, subscale range 0-21, total HADS-t range 0-42; cutoffs ≥8 possible case, ≥11 definite case
EORTC QLQ-C30 (Czech version) — quality of life; functional scales used: physical, role, emotional, cognitive, social function; global health status/QoL scale; symptom items: pain, nausea and vomiting
Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) scale — 0 (death) to 100 (normal condition), illness severity

variables:
sociodemographic ->
gender, marital status, living situation (alone, with partner, with children, with other relatives, in institution), having children (yes/no), religion (yes/no)

clinical ->
cancer diagnosis site (mouth/pharynx, gastrointestinal tract, respiratory tract, bone/cartilage, skin/soft tissues, breast/female reproductive organs, male reproductive organs, urinary bladder, eye/CNS, hematopoiesis, others), Karnofsky Performance Status score, pain

outcome variables ->
HADS-a (anxiety score, 0-21)
HADS-d (depression score, 0-21)
HADS-t (total score, 0-42)
EORTC QLQ-C30 functional/QoL scales

Sample characteristics (n=225): mean age 65.1 years (SD 12.6; range 31-93)

Overall prevalence:
Anxiety (HADS-a ≥8): 33.9% (n=76/225); moderate-severe (HADS-a ≥11): 14.3% (n=32/225); mean anxiety score 6.1 (SD 3.9, median 5)
Depression (HADS-d ≥8): 47.6% (n=107/225); moderate-severe (HADS-d ≥11): 22.3% (n=50/225); mean depression score 7.2 (SD 4.1, median 7)
Total HADS-t ≥16: 46.7% (n=105/225)

Quality of life comparison (Figures 1-2, box plots): patients WITH depression (HADS-d ≥8) and WITH anxiety (HADS-a ≥8) both showed significantly lower overall QoL than patients without (p<0.001 for both).

Key findings for model training:
- Depression (47.6%) more prevalent than anxiety (33.9%) in this population — consistent with general palliative-care literature pattern cited by authors
- Both anxiety and depression correlate strongly with EVERY EORTC QLQ-C30 functional dimension (r range 0.31-0.63), and the association survives adjustment for pain and illness severity — key evidence that psychological state acts as an independent driver of quality of life, not merely a symptom co-occurring with physical decline
- Emotional functioning shows the strongest correlation with anxiety (r=-0.63) — the single strongest correlation in the entire matrix
- Anxiety and depression WORSENED significantly over just 21 days of hospitalization in the repeated-measures subgroup — suggests psychological state should be modeled as time-dependent/deteriorating rather than static, relevant for a recommendation engine that may need to re-assess patients periodically
- No significant relationship found between age and either anxiety or depression — contradicts some other studies in the corpus reporting age as a risk factor; useful as a counter-example for model regularization
- Living with a partner and non-religious status were both associated with HIGHER anxiety (opposite of intuitive assumption that partnership/companionship reduces distress) — authors note this pattern is consistent with some other studies but not universal
- Weak/no correlation between physical symptoms (pain, nausea) and HADS scores relative to the strength of the QoL-functional correlations — supports the authors' framing that psychological distress is not simply a proxy for symptom burden
- Nurses tend to underestimate depression severity in more severely depressed patients (cited from McDonald et al. 1999) — relevant caveat for caregiver-reported data pipelines (ACOMPAÑAR's caregiver-entry model), since caregiver/staff assessments may systematically underestimate severity at the high end

Limitations noted by authors:
- Cross-sectional primary design (with only a partial 21-day repeated-measures subsample of 140/225, due to death or health deterioration in the remainder) — limits generalizability of the longitudinal finding
- No Czech population norms available for HADS at the time of publication; comparison relied on Dutch general-population norms (mean depression score 3.7) as reference, which may not be fully appropriate cross-culturally
- EORTC QLQ-C30 Czech version psychometric properties not independently published/validated at time of study
- Single-center study (University Hospital Ostrava)
- Attrition in the second assessment (85/225 patients lost to death or inability to complete the form) — likely reflects a survivorship bias toward relatively less-severe cases in the repeated-measures comparison
