Article #26
Quality of Life for Hong Kong Chinese Patients with Advanced Gynecological Cancers in the Palliative Phase of Care: A Cross-Sectional Study
Kwok Ying Chan, Man Lui Chan, Thomas Chung Cheung Yau, Cho Wing Li, Hon Wai Cheng, Mau Kwong Sham
Hong Kong, China, published 2012 — Journal of Palliative Care, 28(4):259-266, doi: 10.1177/082585971202800404
53 consecutive patients with advanced gynecological cancer (AGC), Palliative Medical Unit, Grantham Hospital, Hong Kong (cross-sectional study, recruited July 3, 2008 - December 3, 2009)

Instruments:
McGill Quality of Life Questionnaire for Hong Kong Chinese (MQOL-HK) — 17-item patient-rated QOL for palliative cancer patients; 5 domains: physical well-being, psychological well-being, existential well-being, support, sexual function; 0-10 scale per item, 0=worst, 10=best; Cronbach's alpha 0.68-0.85; test-retest reliability 0.75
Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) — Cantonese Chinese version (Cronbach's α=0.77, sensitivity 0.79, specificity 0.80); 7 items each subscale, 0-3 per item, range 0-21; cutoff 8-10 = doubtful case, ≥11 = definite case, ≥15 = severe depression/anxiety suspected
Palliative Performance Scale (PPS) — observer-rated, 0-100% in 10% increments, 5 parameters (ambulation, activity/disease evidence, self-care, intake, level of consciousness)
Psychosocial Adjustment to Illness Scale (PAIS), sexual relationships subscale — 6 items, 0-3 scale (0=no disturbance, 3=marked disturbance), range 0-18, higher = more sexual dysfunction; only administered to patients in a sexual relationship (n=31/53)

variables:
sociodemographic ->
age, marital status, number of children, education years/level, religion, monthly income (HKD), accommodation (residential care facility, alone, with family)

clinical ->
gynecological cancer site (ovary, cervix, uterus), disease stage (III, IV), oncological treatment type/history, past medical history (hypertension, diabetes, cerebrovascular accident, ischemic heart disease, cancer), past psychiatric history, analgesic use, other medications (antihypertensives, CHF/IHD meds, oral hypoglycemics/insulin, antidepressants), PPS score

outcome/psychological variables ->
MQOL-HK total score and 5 domain scores (physical, psychological, existential, support, sexual)
HADS depression subscale score
HADS anxiety subscale score
PAIS sexual relationships subscale score

Prevalence findings:
Depression symptoms (HADS-d >10): 62% (n=33/53); severe depression (HADS-d ≥15): 19% (n=10/53) — only 3 patients were on antidepressants prior to admission
Significant anxiety symptoms: 42% of patients

Multivariate regression (predictors of MQOL-HK total score):
Model included age, HADS depression, HADS anxiety — age and depression were retained as independent significant predictors, together explaining 58.2% of variance in MQOL-HK score
  Age: β = 0.593 (p<0.05) — older age associated with BETTER QOL
  HADS depression: β = -0.286 (p<0.05) — higher depression associated with WORSE QOL
  (HADS anxiety dropped out as non-independent predictor after adjustment)

Key findings for model training:
- Depression prevalence (62%) is markedly higher than most other studies in this corpus — reflects a specific high-symptom-burden population (advanced/Stage IV gynecological cancer, palliative inpatient unit, median PPS only 40%)
- Younger age is consistently and counterintuitively associated with WORSE QOL and (implicitly) greater psychological distress — authors attribute this to unmet life-role expectations and a larger gap between hopes for the future and current experience in younger patients; this is a recurring pattern worth testing against your dataset (may interact with your `age`/`age_group` dimension differently than assumed)
- Physical domain scored worst of all MQOL-HK domains (mean 3.99/10) — pain (72%), edema (52%), fatigue (45%) most prevalent physical symptoms; notably only 50.9% of patients had received any analgesic despite high pain prevalence — a clinician/caregiver detection gap directly relevant to ACOMPAÑAR's alert-generation logic
- Existential domain showed low scores specifically on "life seems purposeful/meaningful" and "feeling good about oneself" — relevant to Logotherapy-axis features in your therapeutic recommendation engine
- Sexual functioning domain was low-scoring but "relationship with partner" was the LEAST affected PAIS item — suggests partner relationship quality is somewhat protective/resilient even when other domains deteriorate; relevant to your "relational" dimension
- Severe under-treatment of depression observed: 62% had depression symptoms but only 3/53 (5.7%) were on antidepressants — reinforces a cross-study pattern (also seen in Article 25/Saudi study) of depression being clinically under-recognized in palliative settings
- PPS (functional performance) correlated only with the physical domain, not with psychological, existential, or support domains — suggests functional status and emotional/existential distress are relatively independent axes, supporting a multidimensional (not purely physical) recommendation model
- Cultural framing note: authors discuss Chinese cultural beliefs (Confucian/Taoist yin-yang framework) around gynecological illness as a source of additional psychological burden and stigma — a qualitative factor not captured in standard scales but relevant if you expand religiosity/cultural-context variables

Limitations noted by authors:
- Cross-sectional design — no causal relationship between clinical correlates and QOL can be established
- Small sample size (n=53), heterogeneous AGC diagnoses (ovarian, cervical, uterine)
- High exclusion rate for PAIS sexual subscale (42% excluded, no active sexual partner) limited interpretability of sexual functioning findings
- HADS is a screening tool, not a definitive diagnostic instrument for depression
- Single-center, single-ethnicity (Hong Kong Cantonese Chinese) population — limits generalizability across cultures
