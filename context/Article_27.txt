Article #27
Symptom distress in advanced cancer patients with anxiety and depression in the palliative care setting
Marvin Delgado-Guay, Henrique A. Parsons, Zhijun Li, J. Lynn Palmer, Eduardo Bruera
United States (MD Anderson Cancer Center group), published 13 November 2008 — Supportive Care in Cancer, 17:573-579 (2009)
216 patients with advanced cancer (retrospective chart review of baseline data pooled from three previous clinical trials conducted by the same palliative care research group)

Instruments:
Edmonton Symptom Assessment System (ESAS) — 10 items, 0-10 scale (10=worst imaginable intensity), assesses physical/psychological symptoms and global well-being; validated as a mood-disorder screening tool at cutoff ≥2/10
Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) — HADS-A (anxiety) and HADS-D (depression) subscales, 0-21 each; cutoff ≥8 for diagnosis; HADS-A: sensitivity 0.89, specificity 0.75, AUC 0.88 (76% correctly classified vs. DSM-IV generalized anxiety disorder via structured interview); HADS-D: sensitivity 0.80, specificity 0.88, AUC 0.93 (87% correctly classified vs. DSM-IV major depressive episode)

variables:
sociodemographic ->
age, sex, race (White, African American, Hispanic, Asian, Other)

clinical ->
primary cancer diagnosis (lung, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, breast, gynecological, hematologic, head and neck, other)

ESAS symptoms (0-10 scale, frequency defined as score ≥1) ->
appetite (poor), drowsiness, fatigue, nausea, pain, dyspnea, well-being (worse), anxiety (ESAS item), depression (ESAS item)

outcome variables ->
HADS-A (anxiety, dichotomized ≥8/<8)
HADS-D (depression, dichotomized ≥8/<8)
combined anxiety+depression status

Overall prevalence:
Depressive mood (HADS-D ≥8): 37% (n=79/216)
Anxiety (HADS-A ≥8): 44% (n=94/216)

Correlation analysis (Spearman):
HADS-A × ESAS-Anxiety item: r=0.56 (p<0.001)
HADS-D × ESAS-Depression item: r=0.39 (p<0.001)
HADS-A × other ESAS symptoms (pain, fatigue, nausea, dyspnea, drowsiness, appetite, well-being): moderate correlation r=0.25-0.5 (p<0.0001) across the board
HADS-D × same symptom set: similar moderate correlation, EXCEPT low correlation with dyspnea (r<0.25, p=0.0031)

Key findings for model training:
- Distinct symptom "signatures" differentiate anxiety from depression: anxiety patients show elevated frequency/intensity of nausea, pain, dyspnea; depression patients show elevated frequency/intensity of drowsiness, fatigue, poor appetite, worse well-being — supports using granular ESAS-style symptom vectors rather than a single "distress" scalar in your classifier's emotional/physical dimensions
- Fatigue is present at very high frequency (94-97%) REGARDLESS of mood status — i.e., fatigue alone is a poor discriminator for depression/anxiety in this population and should be weighted cautiously as a standalone predictor feature
- Comorbid anxiety+depression produces the strongest symptom burden across nearly all ESAS items (compounding effect) — relevant if your algorithm scores a combined "affective burden" feature rather than only individual anxiety/depression variables
- Worse well-being had the strongest/most consistent association with both anxiety and depression across frequency and intensity analyses — potentially a useful single "flagging" item for alert-panel logic if granular ESAS data isn't available
- Bidirectional uncertainty acknowledged by authors: unclear whether mood disorders amplify physical symptom perception (somatization) or whether unmanaged physical symptoms drive mood disorder — causal direction cannot be established from this cross-sectional/retrospective design, relevant caveat for interpreting any of your model's "risk factor" features as causal
- Authors note symptom-mood associations may weaken as death approaches (citing conflicting findings from Teunissen et al., who found NO correlation between physical symptoms and mood in a separate hospitalized cohort) — disease trajectory/proximity to death may moderate the strength of physical-emotional correlations, a potential interaction term worth testing
- Prevalence rates in this study (37% depression, 44% anxiety) sit centrally within the wide range reported across the literature (depression 3-69% per cited studies) — reinforces the need for multi-study aggregation with tool/setting metadata rather than treating single-study prevalence rates as ground truth
- ESAS is proposed by the authors as a practical low-burden depression/anxiety screening tool (cutoff ≥2/10 on its embedded anxiety/depression items) — relevant to ACOMPAÑAR's caregiver-facing observation forms, since it requires minimal patient/caregiver effort compared to full HADS administration

Limitations noted by authors:
- Retrospective chart review — cannot establish causal association between mood disorders and physical symptom intensity
- No structured DSM-IV clinical interviews performed; diagnosis relied solely on HADS cutoff scores
- Data pooled from three distinct prior clinical trials (opioid/hypogonadism study, dyspnea characterization study, spirituality/locus-of-control study) — potential selection bias, though authors report no statistically significant difference in anxiety/depression frequency between the three source studies
- Cross-sectional/baseline-only data — cannot capture how symptom-mood associations may change over the disease trajectory or as death approaches
- Single research group / single institution origin for all three source trials, which may limit generalizability
