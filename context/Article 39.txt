Spiritual Pain Is Associated with Decreased Quality of Life in Advanced Cancer Patients in Palliative Care: An Exploratory Study
Pedro E. Pérez-Cruz, Paola Langer, Cecilia Carrasco, Pilar Bonati, Bogomila Batic, Laura Tupper Satt, Marcela Gonzalez Otaiza
Single-center study (Palliative Care clinic, Complejo Asistencial Dr. Sotero del Rio, Puente Alto, Chile; academic affiliation Pontificia Universidad Catolica de Chile, Santiago), published 2019 — Journal of Palliative Medicine, Vol. 22, No. 6, pp. 663-669, doi: 10.1089/jpm.2018.0340. Epub 16 Jan 2019.
208 cancer patients enrolled at a palliative care (PC) clinic in Puente Alto, Chile, as part of a larger longitudinal study characterizing patients' end of life; cross-sectional baseline analysis. Inclusion criteria: age >=18, having a primary caregiver, no delirium, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) <=80.

Instruments:
Spiritual Pain (SP) — single-item question, 0-10 numeric scale, patient self-report; also single-item measures for financial distress and other spirituality-related variables
Quality of Life (QOL) — 0-100 scale (specific instrument name not confirmed in available abstract/secondary sources; described only as "questionnaires to assess QOL, 0-100")
Global Distress Score-Physical — used to assess physical symptom distress (ESAS-derived composite, exact item set not confirmed in available sources)
Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) — used to assess psychological distress; HADS-D subscale is the depression outcome reported in the source review table (Deng et al. 2026, Table 1)
Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) — used as inclusion criterion (<=80) and likely covariate
Demographic and spirituality/religiosity variables collected at baseline (exact list not confirmed from available sources)

variables:

Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS, <=80 required for inclusion), cancer diagnosis (mixed cancer types, per source review table), absence of delirium (inclusion criterion)

Reported sample characteristics (from PubMed abstract):
208 patients enrolled; mean age 64 years; 50% female; 67% reported spiritual pain (SP present, defined as SP score >0 on the 0-10 scale, consistent with usage in related Latin American SP literature).

Reported association: Spiritual pain was significantly associated with decreased quality of life in baseline analyses; the association with QOL was adjusted for potential confounders in a multivariable model (specific covariates and effect sizes not available from accessible sources).

Prevalence (per source review table, Deng et al. 2026, Table 1): Depression (HADS-D) prevalence in this sample: 37.5%. Sample: n=208, mean age 64.0 +/- 14.0, % female 50%, mixed cancer types.

Key findings for model training:
- This is one of the few studies in your corpus that centers the SPIRITUAL/EXISTENTIAL dimension (spiritual pain, single-item 0-10 self-report) as a driver of quality of life in advanced cancer, rather than depression itself as the primary outcome — depression (HADS-D) appears here as a secondary/comorbid distress measure rather than the study's main focus. Relevant to ACOMPANAR's spiritual dimension, and a useful reminder that spiritual pain and depression are related but conceptually distinct constructs that this study measured separately.
- High prevalence of spiritual pain (67%) in a Chilean PC population — consistent with other Latin American studies in the broader literature (which report SP prevalence in the 44-67% range) — suggesting the spiritual dimension may carry substantial independent weight in this cultural/regional context, worth weighting appropriately in a model calibrated for Spanish-speaking/Latin American populations.
- Inclusion criteria required a primary caregiver and excluded patients with delirium and KPS >80 — the sample is therefore restricted to patients with a support person and more advanced functional impairment; results may not generalize to patients without caregiver support or with better functional status.
- The single-item (0-10) measurement approach for spiritual pain is a notably lightweight instrument compared to multi-item spirituality scales (e.g., FACIT-Sp) used elsewhere in the literature — a methodological consideration if standardizing measurement approaches across your corpus.

Limitations noted by authors / inferred:
- Cross-sectional design (baseline analysis of a longitudinal study) — cannot establish causal direction between spiritual pain and QOL.
- Single-item measurement of spiritual pain — exploratory/screening-level instrument, not a validated multi-item spirituality or existential distress scale; may lack nuance relative to standardized tools.
- Single-center sample (one PC clinic in Puente Alto, Chile) — limits generalizability, though study is explicitly framed by the authors as "exploratory."
- Restrictive inclusion criteria (requires a primary caregiver, excludes delirium, KPS <=80 only) — selection bias toward a specific subgroup of PC patients; may not represent patients with better functional status, no caregiver, or cognitive impairment.
- Full text not accessible for this extraction — exact QOL instrument name, full confounder-adjusted regression table, and complete baseline characteristics table could not be verified independently; entry is based on abstract-level and secondary-source data only.
